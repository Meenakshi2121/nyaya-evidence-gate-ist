# Final Analysis Specification

**Project:** Nyāya-inspired evidence gate for individualised treatment recommendations  
**Case study:** Aspirin in suspected acute ischaemic stroke using the International Stroke Trial  
**Specification locked:** 13 October 2026  
**Status:** Final specification following a disclosed exploratory analysis and a data-coding audit

## 1. Purpose and provenance

This document fixes the analysis decisions used to evaluate the evidence gate before the final policy-performance analysis is run. An earlier exploratory analysis used a different gate specification and produced preliminary findings, including approximately 47.1% gate coverage and no clear improvement over treating all patients with aspirin. Those results informed development of the framework. The analysis specified here is therefore not described as prospectively prespecified. Any subsequent deviation from this document will be dated, justified and reported.

The data audit, variable coding checks and missingness audit undertaken before this lock are not treated as policy-performance analyses. They established the outcome coding, treatment coding, heparin grouping, analytic sample and operational definition of observed-data adequacy.

## 2. Study population, treatment and outcome

The source is version 2 of the International Stroke Trial database (DOI: 10.7488/ds/104). The trial randomised 19,435 patients with suspected acute ischaemic stroke. The primary analytic sample contains 19,285 patients with a classifiable six-month outcome. Patients with outcome codes 0 or 9 are excluded from model training and policy evaluation.

The treatment is randomised aspirin allocation (`RXASP`), coded as aspirin versus no aspirin. The nominal aspirin-allocation probability is fixed at 0.5 for policy evaluation. The adverse outcome is death or dependency at six months: `OCCODE` values 1 or 2 are adverse, while values 3 or 4 are alive and independent for this binary endpoint.

Heparin allocation is handled as a factorial-trial covariate. `N` denotes no heparin, `L` low-dose heparin, and `M` or pilot code `H` higher-dose heparin. The primary models adjust for this three-level variable. Aspirin–heparin interaction is examined as a sensitivity analysis.

## 3. Baseline variables and missing information

The prespecified baseline variables are `RDELAY`, `RCONSC`, `SEX`, `AGE`, `RSLEEP`, `RATRIAL`, `RCT`, `RVISINF`, `RHEP24`, `RASP3`, `RSBP`, `RDEF1`–`RDEF8`, and `STYPE`. Hospital number is not treated as a patient identifier or model predictor. A stable study-row identifier is generated solely for reproducible joins and tie-breaking.

All preprocessing is learned within each training fold. Numeric variables use training-fold median imputation; categorical variables use training-fold modal imputation. Missingness indicators are added, categorical levels are encoded without learning from the held-out fold, and scaling is applied where required by the penalised model.

The missingness audit found that 18,304 of 19,285 analytic patients have all essential variables observed and 981 lack essential information. `RATRIAL` was not collected in the trial's pilot phase. Structurally unavailable information still constitutes unavailable patient-level evidence for the gate. These patients remain available for model fitting after fold-specific imputation but fail the data-adequacy check.

## 4. Cross-fitting and treatment-effect estimators

The primary analysis uses five-fold cross-fitting with seed 20261012, stratified jointly by aspirin allocation and observed outcome. Every recommendation, gate check, uncertainty score and outcome-regression prediction for a patient is produced without fitting on that patient's held-out fold.

The primary individualised recommendation is generated by a causal forest. Stability is assessed across three estimators fixed before the final run:

1. causal forest;
2. L2-penalised logistic regression containing treatment–covariate interactions; and
3. a gradient-boosted T-learner fitted separately in the aspirin and no-aspirin groups.

All treatment effects are expressed as differences in predicted probability of death or dependency under aspirin minus no aspirin. A negative estimate therefore recommends aspirin; zero or a positive estimate recommends no aspirin. Model settings are recorded in `config/analysis.yml`.

## 5. Non-compensatory evidence gate

For patient *i*, the gate is

`G_i = D_i × R_i × S_i`,

where each component is binary. Failure of any component requires deferral and cannot be offset by strength elsewhere.

### 5.1 Data adequacy (`D_i`)

The check passes only when every prespecified essential baseline variable is observed and its value is permitted by the trial dictionary. Imputation permits model fitting but does not convert missing observed evidence into a gate pass.

### 5.2 Reference support (`R_i`)

Reference support is assessed within each training fold using Gower distance. For each held-out patient, the mean distance to the 50 nearest training patients is calculated separately in the aspirin and no-aspirin arms. The support score is the worse of the two arm-specific mean distances. The check passes when this score does not exceed the training-fold leave-one-out 95th percentile. This primarily identifies covariate outliers; the randomised allocation means arm-specific sparsity is expected to be uncommon.

### 5.3 Estimator agreement (`S_i`)

The check passes only when all three prespecified estimators recommend the same action. Non-finite estimates fail. The rule assesses disagreement in the recommended action; it does not use a clinical materiality margin.

## 6. Policies and matched-coverage comparator

Four policies are evaluated:

1. **Treat all:** every patient receives aspirin.
2. **Ungated individualisation:** every patient receives the causal-forest action.
3. **Gated individualisation:** accepted patients receive the causal-forest action; deferred patients receive aspirin.
4. **Uncertainty-only abstention:** patients accepted under a causal-forest standard-error threshold receive the causal-forest action; deferred patients receive aspirin.

For a fair comparison, the uncertainty threshold is selected separately within each held-out fold so that uncertainty-only abstention accepts the same number of patients as the full gate. Ties are resolved using the generated study-row identifier. A secondary, unmatched sensitivity analysis defers when the causal forest's 95% treatment-effect interval crosses zero.

## 7. Policy evaluation and estimands

Expected six-month risk of death or dependency is estimated with cross-fitted augmented inverse-probability weighting; lower risk is better. Cross-fitted predictions from the gradient-boosted T-learner provide the outcome regressions. The aspirin propensity is fixed at the nominal randomisation probability of 0.5. Reuse of a cross-fitted outcome model affects precision rather than identification because the treatment propensity is supplied by the randomised design.

The analysis reports expected risk for all four policies and risk differences comparing the gated policy with treat-all, ungated individualisation and uncertainty-only abstention at matched coverage.

Within accepted and deferred patients separately, gain from individualisation is defined as:

`risk under treat-all aspirin − risk under ungated individualisation`.

A positive gain favours individualisation. The principal gate-validity contrast is accepted-group gain minus deferred-group gain. The number and proportion recommended no aspirin are reported separately for accepted and deferred patients because individualisation differs from the default only for those recommendations.

## 8. Inference and robustness analyses

Ninety-five per cent percentile confidence intervals for the principal contrast and policy comparisons are obtained from 1,000 bootstrap replicates stratified jointly by aspirin allocation and outcome. Each replicate repeats fold assignment, imputation, model fitting, gate construction, matched-coverage selection and policy evaluation so that uncertainty from the data-dependent gate and policy is represented.

The primary partition is supplemented by 20 prespecified alternative fold partitions. The median and range of key estimates are reported, together with each patient's frequency of gate acceptance. These partitions are sensitivity analyses and do not replace the primary seeded analysis.

Additional sensitivity analyses examine the aspirin–heparin interaction and the unmatched rule that defers when the causal forest's 95% treatment-effect interval crosses zero.

## 9. Reporting and deviations

The report will include the pass/fail count for each gate component, overlap among failures, overall and fold-specific coverage, stability across partitions, no-aspirin recommendation counts, all policy risks, risk differences and confidence intervals. Patient-level data and patient-level model outputs will not be committed to the public repository.

Any change after the commit that locks this document must be recorded in a dated deviation log before rerunning the affected analysis. The rationale and effect of the change will be reported in the manuscript or supplement.
